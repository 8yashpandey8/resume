Sonakshi had a habit of judging people too quickly.

If someone sounded arrogant, she avoided them.
If someone appeared too confident, she assumed they lacked depth.

At university she met a student named Aarav during a literature seminar. He was intelligent, calm, and extremely reserved. Their conversations usually turned into arguments because Sonakshi believed he looked down on everyone around him.

Over time their rivalry became famous among classmates.

One semester they were assigned the same research project and were forced to work together. Sonakshi expected frustration, but instead she slowly discovered things she had never noticed before.

Aarav worked nights at a bookstore to support his education.
He avoided social gatherings because he was caring for his ill mother.
And despite his quiet personality, he remembered small details about everyone around him.

For the first time Sonakshi realized how easily people create false versions of others based only on appearances.

That realization disturbed her because she understood she had done exactly the same thing.

As weeks passed, their conversations became more honest and less defensive. Instead of trying to win arguments, she began listening.

The experience transformed the way she viewed relationships and human behavior.

Years later she would remember that phase of life not as a romantic story, but as the moment she learned emotional maturity.

Like many characters from classic novels, Sonakshi discovered that pride often hides insecurity, and understanding another person requires patience far beyond first impressions.
